# Revenue Pivot Proposal: "InboxOps" (AI Workflow Copilot for Service Businesses)

## Direct answer: how confident am I?
**Confidence in this product direction: 8/10** for generating income within 90 days, **if we execute as a services-led wedge first** (paid setup + managed automation), then transition into SaaS.

Why not 10/10?
- SMB inbox workflows are painful and valuable, but distribution is hard.
- Pure SaaS takes longer to trust and adopt.
- The fastest cash path is productized service + software, not software-only.

## The best-income strategy (recommended)
Build **InboxOps** as a **hybrid model**:
1. **Phase 1 (cash now):** Done-for-you inbox triage/follow-up setup for one niche.
2. **Phase 2 (scale):** Convert repeated workflows into self-serve product features.
3. **Phase 3 (retention):** Expand from lead handling to quoting, scheduling, and reactivation.

This gives us near-term revenue while we learn what the product must automate.

## What I would build
A narrow, monetizable system for **small service businesses** (home services, small agencies, med spas/clinics, legal/finance boutiques) that turns inbound messages into revenue-generating follow-through.

Core value:
- Parse leads/requests from Gmail + forms.
- Auto-classify: hot lead, existing customer, billing, scheduling, spam.
- Draft context-aware replies in brand voice.
- Extract tasks and deadlines into a simple pipeline.
- Trigger automated follow-up until booked/lost.

Positioning: **"Stop losing leads in your inbox."**

## Why this over other ideas
Compared to generic AI assistants, chatbots, and horizontal CRMs:
- ROI is immediate and measurable (response time, booked jobs, recovered leads).
- The buyer already feels the pain weekly.
- MVP can be built with known integrations and simple UI.
- Expansion paths are clear (more inboxes, more automations, more workflow modules).

## Ideal first customer profile (strict)
- 3–20 employees.
- 30+ inbound requests/week.
- Average deal value > $500.
- Owner still involved in sales follow-up.
- Current process = inbox + spreadsheets + missed callbacks.

If a prospect does not meet these, do not prioritize them in the first 20 customers.

## Offer design (what we sell first)
### Offer A — "Inbox Recovery Sprint" (fast cash)
- One-time implementation: **$1,500–$3,500**.
- 10-day deployment.
- Includes triage rules, reply templates, follow-up sequences, dashboard.

### Offer B — "InboxOps Platform" (recurring)
- Starter: **$99/mo** (1 inbox, 500 msgs).
- Growth: **$249/mo** (5 inboxes, shared pipeline).
- Pro: **$599/mo** (20 inboxes, advanced rules, priority support).

### Offer C — Managed ops add-on (high-margin)
- **$500–$2,000/mo** for monthly optimization, prompt tuning, and conversion reviews.

## 4-week MVP scope
1. Gmail OAuth + message sync.
2. Rule + AI hybrid classification.
3. Draft reply generator with approval mode.
4. Task extraction + Kanban board.
5. Follow-up scheduler.
6. KPI view: first-response time, follow-up rate, booked value.

Non-goals for MVP:
- Full CRM replacement.
- Multi-channel social/WhatsApp support.
- Fully autonomous send by default.

## Go-to-market with highest probability of income
1. Pick one vertical: **home services** first (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, electrical).
2. Sell 5 paid implementation sprints manually via cold email + Loom audits.
3. Use those 5 accounts as design partners and proof points.
4. Publish concrete case studies with dollar outcomes.
5. Layer in SaaS subscriptions and managed add-ons.

Outbound hook:
- "We found X unanswered quote requests in your process. We can recover them in 10 days."

## Unit economics target
- CAC payback < 2 months.
- Gross margin > 75% by month 6.
- Implementation attach rate > 40% of new customers.
- Churn < 4% monthly in first niche.

## Risks and mitigation
- **Trust risk with AI replies** → approval-required by default; one-click edit/send.
- **Integration complexity** → Gmail first; delay Outlook until 10 paying customers.
- **Low willingness to pay** → lead with recovered revenue proof, not AI novelty.
- **Founder bandwidth** → productized onboarding checklist and templates.

## 90-day execution plan
- **Days 1–14:** 25 customer interviews, niche lock-in, simple landing page, manual audit script.
- **Days 15–30:** MVP + first 3 paid implementations.
- **Days 31–60:** reach 10 paying customers, tighten onboarding, collect 3 public case studies.
- **Days 61–90:** hit 20 paying customers, launch referral/channel partners, raise prices for new cohorts.

## Kill criteria (to protect time and money)
If by day 45 we do not have:
- at least 3 paying customers,
- at least one case study showing recovered revenue,
- and weekly usage from >60% of seats,

then pivot to adjacent higher-ticket workflow (e.g., quoting + scheduling automation) using the same base infrastructure.

## Final stance
If the goal is **best chance of real income quickly**, I would still choose InboxOps—**but execute it as a revenue-first hybrid (implementation + SaaS), not a pure app launch**.
